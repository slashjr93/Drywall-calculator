How Do You Calculate How Much Drywall You Need?

To estimate how many sheets of drywall you need, first calculate the total square footage of the surfaces you plan to cover. This usually includes the walls and, if needed, the ceiling.

After calculating the surface area, subtract the area of doors and windows. Then add an allowance for waste from cutting, damaged pieces, and the layout of the room.

Finally, divide the total square footage by the square footage covered by one drywall sheet and round up to the next whole sheet.

Standard Drywall Sheet Sizes

A common drywall sheet is 4 feet wide. Several lengths are available.

  • 4 × 8 feet: 32 square feet
  • 4 × 10 feet: 40 square feet
  • 4 × 12 feet: 48 square feet

The best size depends on the room and how the sheets can be transported and installed. Larger sheets can reduce the number of seams, but they can be more difficult to handle.

Drywall Calculation Formula

Wall Area = Room Perimeter × Wall Height

Net Area = Wall Area − Doors − Windows

Total Area = Net Wall Area + Ceiling Area

Total With Waste = Total Area × (1 + Waste Percentage)

Sheets Needed = Total With Waste ÷ Sheet Coverage

Example: 12 × 12 Room

Suppose you have a 12 × 12 foot room with 8-foot walls and you want to drywall all four walls.

Room perimeter: 2 × (12 + 12) = 48 feet

Wall area: 48 × 8 = 384 square feet

If you use 4 × 8 drywall sheets, each sheet covers 32 square feet.

Before waste: 384 ÷ 32 = 12 sheets

With a 10% waste allowance: 384 × 1.10 = 422.4 square feet

422.4 ÷ 32 = 13.2, so you would need approximately 14 sheets.

Actual requirements can vary depending on doors, windows, ceiling coverage, wall height, sheet orientation, and the room layout.

How Much Extra Drywall Should I Buy?

A waste allowance is important because drywall normally requires cutting around corners, doors, windows, electrical boxes, and other obstacles.

For a simple room, 10% waste is a reasonable starting estimate. More complicated rooms may require a larger allowance.

Should I Include the Ceiling?

If you are drywalling the ceiling as well as the walls, calculate the ceiling separately.

For a rectangular room, ceiling area is calculated by multiplying the room length by the room width.

For example, a 12 × 12 foot ceiling has: 12 × 12 = 144 square feet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 12 × 12 room?

It depends on wall height, whether the ceiling is included, doors and windows, sheet size, and waste. For example, a 12 × 12 room with 8-foot walls requires about 14 standard 4 × 8 sheets for the four walls when using a 10% waste allowance and before accounting for openings.

How many square feet does a 4 × 8 sheet of drywall cover?

A 4 × 8 drywall sheet covers 32 square feet.

Should I buy extra drywall?

Yes. A waste allowance helps account for cuts, damaged pieces, and difficult room layouts. Around 10% is commonly used as a starting estimate for a straightforward project.
