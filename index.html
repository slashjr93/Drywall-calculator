How Many Sheets of Drywall Do I Need?

Use the drywall calculator above to estimate how many sheets of drywall you'll need for a room. Enter the room length, width, and wall height, then choose your drywall sheet size.

The calculator estimates the surface area of the walls and can include the ceiling. It also subtracts doors and windows and adds a waste allowance to account for cutting and unusable pieces.

How Is Drywall Calculated?

Wall area is calculated using the room's perimeter multiplied by the wall height. Door and window areas are then deducted from the wall area. If the ceiling is included, the room length is multiplied by the room width to determine the ceiling area.

Finally, the selected waste percentage is added and the result is divided by the square footage covered by one drywall sheet. The result is rounded up to the next whole sheet.

Standard Drywall Sheet Sizes

  • 4 × 8 foot sheet = 32 square feet
  • 4 × 10 foot sheet = 40 square feet
  • 4 × 12 foot sheet = 48 square feet

Larger sheets can reduce the number of seams, but they can also be harder to transport and handle. The best sheet size depends on the room layout and installation conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much drywall do I need for a 12 × 12 room?

The answer depends on the wall height, whether the ceiling is included, the number and size of openings, the drywall sheet size, and the amount of waste you allow. Enter your dimensions above for an estimate.

How much area does a 4 × 8 sheet of drywall cover?

A standard 4 × 8 foot drywall sheet covers 32 square feet before accounting for cuts and waste.

Should I add extra drywall for waste?

Yes. Cuts, damaged pieces, awkward layouts, and installation mistakes can create waste. A 10% allowance is a reasonable starting point for many straightforward projects, although complex layouts may require more.
